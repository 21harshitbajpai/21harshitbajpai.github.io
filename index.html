About

I am a Ph.D. researcher in the Department of Mathematics at IIT Roorkee, supervised by Prof. Ankik Kumar Giri. My thesis is on stochastic and learning-based iterative regularization methods for ill-posed problems, with applications to medical imaging.

My work draws on functional analysis, stochastic optimization and numerical analysis. I study when iterative methods such as Landweber iteration, stochastic gradient descent, mirror descent and iterated Tikhonov converge, how fast, and when to stop them under noise, including heuristic rules that need no knowledge of the noise level.

Before my Ph.D., I worked as a Research Scientist at Arithmic Labs, Bengaluru. I am a member of the Inverse Problems Young Academy (2026–2029). My collaborators are listed under Research.

I am happy to talk about research and collaboration. Get in touch through the contact details below.

Research

Regularization theory for ill-posed inverse problems

An inverse problem asks for an unknown x from data yδ = F(x) + noise. When the problem is ill-posed, small errors in the data cause large errors in the solution, so the method has to be regularized. My research designs iterative methods that regularize through early stopping, proves their convergence and rates in Hilbert and Banach spaces, and tests them on tomography and imaging problems.

Stochastic iterative methods

Stochastic gradient descent, Kaczmarz-type and block coordinate methods that use one equation or block per step, scaling to large systems while keeping regularizing behaviour.

Graph and data-driven priors

Graph Laplacians built from training data, plug-in denoisers and learned operators that steer the iteration toward realistic images.

When to stop

A priori, a posteriori (discrepancy) and heuristic stopping rules, including Hanke–Raus type rules that work without knowing the noise level.

15 Feb 2025

On the convergence of iteratively regularized stochastic gradient descent for solving nonlinear inverse problems

Annual Inverse Meet, IIT Gandhinagar
Abstract

A variant of stochastic gradient descent, iteratively regularized SGD (IRSGD), for nonlinear ill-posed problems in Hilbert spaces. Without noise, the mean-square iteration error converges to zero. With noisy data, a heuristic parameter choice rule in the spirit of Hanke and Raus selects the stopping index without knowing the noise level; the method then stops after finitely many steps and remains a regularization method. Convergence rates follow under standard source conditions, without assuming polynomially decaying step sizes. Numerical tests cover first-kind integral equations and Schlieren tomography.

31 Aug 2024

Stochastic data-driven Bouligand–Landweber method for non-smooth inverse problems

Research Scholars Day, IIT Roorkee
Abstract

A stochastic gradient variant for systems of non-smooth ill-posed equations that uses training data through a bounded linear operator to guide the iteration. Each step picks one equation at random together with a data-driven term. For exact data the mean-square error converges to zero; for noisy data an a priori stopping rule gives a regularization method. Convergence and stability are proved in infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and illustrated numerically.
